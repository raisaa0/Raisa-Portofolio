ANALYSIS OF SBI INTEREST RATES, EXCHANGE RATES, FIRM SIZE, DER, AND INFLATION ON CORPORATE BOND YIELDS

Panel Data Random Effect Model Robust SE
01

Introduction

1.1. Background

The capital market plays a vital role in a country's economy, acting as a funding source for companies and an investment vehicle for society. In Indonesia, corporate bonds are increasingly popular. For investors, the main indicator is the yield (expected return for the risk borne). Yield movements are highly dynamic and influenced by market risk perception. Higher risk demands a higher yield (risk premium), which in turn increases the issuer's cost of debt.

Fluctuations in corporate bond yields are influenced by macroeconomic factors (external) and firm fundamentals (internal). Bank Indonesia's monetary policy via the SBI rate is the main benchmark. Rising SBI rates push market interest rates up, forcing investors to demand higher bond yields. Additionally, inflation (via the Fisher Effect) erodes real value, forcing investors to demand higher nominal returns. Rupiah exchange rate stability against USD is also a key indicator of market sentiment.

1.2. Research Questions

  1. How do SBI rates, Exchange Rate (ER), Firm Size, DER, and Inflation partially affect Corporate Bond Yields?
  2. Which panel data estimation model (CEM, FEM, or REM) is the most appropriate?
02

Theoretical Framework

Fisher Effect

Nominal interest rate (R) is the sum of the real interest rate (r) and expected inflation (πe).

R = r + πe

Signaling Theory

Large Firm Size and low DER signal good credit quality. Large firms (too big to fail) have lower default risk, so investors accept lower yields.

Research Hypotheses

  • H1: SBI rate has a positive and significant effect on Yield.
  • H2: Exchange Rate (ER) has a positive and significant effect on Yield.
  • H3: Firm Size has a negative and significant effect on Yield.
  • H4: DER has a positive and significant effect on Yield.
  • H5: Inflation has a positive and significant effect on Yield.
03

Methodology

This study uses quantitative secondary Panel Data (Time Series: Semester II 2008 – Semester II 2010; Cross-Section: Companies listed on IDX). Data is processed using R Studio.

Variables & Operational Definition

Variable Definition & Measurement
Yield (Y) Yield to Maturity (YTM) expected by investors (%).
SBI Rate (X1) Bank Indonesia Certificate interest rate (1-month tenor) (%).
Exchange Rate (X2) Mid-rate of IDR against USD at the end of the semester.
Firm Size (X3) Total assets, transformed into Natural Logarithm (Ln).
DER (X4) Total Debt to Total Equity ratio.
Inflation (X5) National CPI inflation rate from BPS (%).

Results & Discussion

4.1. Pre-Estimation & Model Selection

Multicollinearity test showed all VIF values < 10 (no multicollinearity). Panel model selection tests (Chow, Hausman, LM) concluded that Random Effect Model (REM) is the most appropriate.

4.2. Outlier Detection & Diagnostic Tests

Using Studentized Residuals, one outlier was detected at observation #107 (Bond MYOR03, 2009 Semester I) with a residual > 3. This is a valid economic phenomenon (high yield due to 2008 crisis) and is retained, but estimation uses Robust Standard Error to minimize bias.

4.4. Partial Interpretation (t-test)

  • SBI: Positive and significant. A 1% increase raises Yield by 1.1151%.
  • Firm Size: Negative and significant. Larger firms offer lower yields due to lower default risk.
  • Inflation: Negative and significant. A 1% increase decreases Yield by 0.4372%.
  • ER & DER: Not statistically significant in this period.

Visualization of Regression Coefficients

4.5. Coefficient of Determination (R²) & Simultaneous Test (F)

Adjusted R-Squared = 0.73289 (73.29%). This means the independent variables explain 73.29% of the variation in Yield. The Wald Chi-Square test (p < 2.2e-16) confirms that all independent variables simultaneously have a significant effect on Yield.

05

Conclusion & Recommendation

5.1. Conclusion

The Random Effect Model (REM) is the most valid estimation method (R² = 73.29%). SBI Rate has a positive and significant effect, Firm Size has a negative and significant effect, and Inflation has a negative and significant effect. Meanwhile, Exchange Rate and DER do not have a significant impact in the 2008-2010 period.

5.2. Recommendations

  1. For Investors: Use the SBI rate as the main benchmark and prioritize bonds from large-asset companies for risk mitigation.
  2. For Issuers: Increase asset accumulation to boost investor trust, allowing the company to issue bonds with lower coupons.
  3. For Future Research: Replace insignificant variables (DER & ER) with more relevant indicators and extend the observation period.
