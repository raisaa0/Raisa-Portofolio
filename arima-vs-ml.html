COMPARISON OF ARIMA AND MACHINE LEARNING FOR BREN STOCK FORECASTING

One-Step-Ahead Evaluation with Diebold-Mariano Test

Time Series ARIMA Machine Learning Diebold-Mariano
01

Introduction

Stock price forecasting remains a debated problem between classical statistical and machine learning approaches. This study compares ARIMA with Random Forest, XGBoost, Support Vector Regression (SVR), and Neural Network Autoregression (NNAR) for forecasting the adjusted closing price of PT Barito Renewables Energy Tbk (BREN.JK). The stock experienced a sharp regime shift, testing the robustness of forecasting models against structural breaks.

To ensure a fair comparison, this research uses (i) one-step-ahead evaluation, (ii) log-return modeling for ML models, (iii) a naive benchmark (random walk), and (iv) the Diebold-Mariano test to formally assess accuracy differences.

02

Methodology

Data Split

  • Total: 649 trading days (Jan 2, 2024 – Sep 30, 2026)
  • Train (80%): 519 days (Jan 2024 – Mar 2026)
  • Test (20%): 130 days (Mar – Sep 2026)

Evaluation Schemes

  • Fixed Parameter: Model estimated once on train data.
  • Walk-Forward: Model re-estimated every 5 trading days.
  • Benchmark: Naive forecast (random walk).

ARIMA Model Selection

The ADF test on the training data showed non-stationarity at level (p = 0.1932). After first differencing, the data became stationary (p < 0.01). Using auto.arima with BIC criteria, ARIMA(1,1,0) was selected. The Ljung-Box test on residuals (p = 0.8384) confirmed no autocorrelation.

ARIMA(1,1,0): Y_t = Y_{t-1} + 0.1476(Y_{t-1} - Y_{t-2}) + ε_t

ML Hyperparameters (Grid Search)

Model Hyperparameters
Random Forest 500 trees; mtry = 3; min node size = 20
XGBoost Max depth = 2; 100 iterations; learning rate = 0.05
SVR (RBF) Cost = 0.1; Gamma = 0.1
NNAR 5 lags; 5 hidden units; 20 initializations

3.2. Accuracy & Diebold-Mariano Test

ARIMA(1,1,0) was the most accurate model (RMSE 221.98; MAPE 3.85%) and the only model significantly better than the naive forecast under both schemes (p = 0.0135). ML models performed around the naive benchmark, with small differences (~10 RMSE points).

04

Conclusion

  • ARIMA(1,1,0) is the most accurate model (RMSE 221.98; MAPE 3.85%) and the only one significantly better than the naive forecast.
  • ARIMA significantly outperforms Random Forest, XGBoost, and SVR on both schemes, and NNAR on the walk-forward scheme (weak evidence).
  • No ML model consistently performs best: NNAR is best under fixed parameters, while SVR and XGBoost are nearly tied under walk-forward.
  • Periodic re-estimation does not meaningfully improve ML accuracy. The complexity of ML models does not guarantee higher accuracy for stocks with structural breaks.
